#+begin_export rst
==========
Surrogates
==========
#+end_export

This tutorial fits an additive surrogate on a Halton pilot, draws proposals from it, and searches a function along one direction with a reduced objective.
The surrogates are Rust-only.
[[file:../explanation/surrogates.org][Surrogates]] explains the models.

* What each call does

- =AdditiveSurrogate::fit(x, y, bounds, degree)= fits =c + sum_j g_j(x_j)=, with each =g_j= a Chebyshev series up to degree =degree= in coordinate =j=.
  The rows of =x= are the pilot points and =y= their values; it needs more rows than =degree=.
  It runs 12 backfitting passes, refitting one coordinate at a time against the residual of the others; =fit_backfit= takes the number of passes.
- =value= and =gradient= evaluate the fit; the surrogate also implements =Objective= and =Gradient=, so any driver can use it in place of the true function.
- =sample(n, temperature, grid_m, &mut rng)= draws =n= points from =exp(-(f - f_min) / T)= of the fit, one coordinate at a time by inverse CDF on a grid of =grid_m= nodes.
  =temperature= is in the units of the fitted values.
  These are proposals: a sampler should accept or reject them with the true objective.
- =ReducedObjective::new(inner, origin, basis, bounds)= evaluates =inner= at =origin + basis r=, where =r= lives in the box =bounds= of dimension =basis.ncols()=.
  Here the basis is the diagonal, so =r = 1= is the point =(1, 1)=.
- =ChebyshevSurrogate::new(bounds, terms, coeffs)= is a total-degree Chebyshev polynomial with given coefficients, for use on the reduced box; eindir does not fit it.

Write =Objective::dim(&reduced)= when the type also implements =Gradient=, since =reduced.dim()= is then ambiguous.
