* Why not random points

Independent uniform points cluster and leave gaps by chance.
A low-discrepancy sequence places each new point where the earlier ones left room, so every box-shaped region holds close to its share of the points.
For smooth integrands this gives quasi-Monte Carlo errors that shrink faster with =n= than the =1/sqrt(n)= of Monte Carlo, and for optimisation and fitting it gives starts and pilot designs that cover the box evenly.
The points are also deterministic: the same arguments give the same design in Rust and in Python, so a benchmark that changes the optimiser does not change its starts as well.

* The Halton sequence

=radical_inverse(k, b)= writes =k= in base =b= and mirrors the digits about the radix point: in base 2, =k = 6 = 110= becomes =0.011 = 0.375=.
Halton point =k= in =d= dimensions takes the radical inverse of =k= in the first =d= primes, one prime per axis [1]; =halton_unit(k, d)= returns it.
=halton_points(bounds, n, skip)= scales points =skip= to =skip + n - 1= into the box, and =low_discrepancy_points= is the same function.
Point 0 is the corner =low=, so =skip = 1= is the usual choice.

eindir grows its prime table on demand, so designs with thousands of axes cost one table extension instead of a search per axis.
Rows are computed in parallel from 64 rows up, with the same result as the serial path.

In many dimensions the Halton sequence has a known weakness: the axes built on two large primes advance almost in step for the first points, so their projection shows lines instead of an even cover.
Skipping more of the sequence, or shifting it, reduces the effect for small =n=.

* Shifted designs

=shifted_low_discrepancy_points(bounds, n, skip, seed)= adds one uniform random vector, drawn from =seed=, to every unit point modulo 1 before scaling.
This is the randomisation of Cranley and Patterson [2].
Each shift keeps the low discrepancy of the design, different seeds give independent replicas, and the spread of an estimate over replicas measures its error.
The shift comes from a seeded =StdRng=, so a seed gives the same points with the same build of the crate; a new version of =rand= may change them.

* Anchored designs

A Halton design after point 0 never contains a corner of the box, and an optimum on the boundary is then reached only by clipping.
=boundary_anchored_low_discrepancy_points= overwrites the first rows: with room for them, the =2^d= corners followed by the centre, and otherwise =low=, =high= and the centre.
The other rows keep their Halton values, so the design remains well spread inside the box.
